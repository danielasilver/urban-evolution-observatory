How The TUEM Hypothesis Space Is Being Explored

A public view of how the Observatory turns a formal model into hypotheses, measurement gaps, evidence priorities, and revised questions.

This public view explains how the formal model is being explored. It does not expose internal control files or private evidence notes.

How To Read This

The formal model supplies a disciplined menu of things to vary: what part of urban life is changing, what evolutionary mechanism is at work, where the change occurs, when it happens, and what kind of relation is expected. The dashboard turns those formal combinations into readable conjectures, then asks what current proxy data can and cannot observe.

1
Picture a real place changing. A neighborhood has physical form, groups of people and institutions, and activities. Some parts endure while others change.
2
Translate that place into TUEM terms. The model asks which component is changing, which evolutionary process is plausible, and what relation should be visible.
3
Check what data can see. Each claim must say whether current data observe the concept directly, partially, or only through a weak proxy.
4
Connect it back to the papers. Part II says what object is being watched, Part III says what rule of change is operating, and Part I keeps the claim tied to real urban context.
5
Rank, hold, scout, or test. The priority queue is not the whole universe. It is the currently highest-scoring slice of explored hypotheses, after clarity and proxy limits are visible.
